How this ovulation calculator works

Ovulation is estimated by counting backwards from the end of your cycle, not forwards from the start, because the second half of the cycle is the more stable part. Here is the exact arithmetic used on this page.

Ovulation day = LMP + (cycle length − 14)
Fertile window = ovulation day − 5 days → ovulation day + 1 day (7 days)
Peak fertility days = ovulation day − 2 days and ovulation day − 1 day
Next period = LMP + cycle length
Suggested test date = ovulation day + 14 days
  • Default assumptions. The calculator assumes a 28-day cycle and a luteal phase (the time from ovulation to the next period) of 14 days, which is the textbook default and the basis of Naegele's rule.
  • Cycle adjustment is automatic. A 35-day cycle puts ovulation on day 21 of the cycle (35 − 14); a 24-day cycle puts it on day 10 (24 − 14). Nothing else is adjusted.
  • Cycle length is counted from day 1 of one period to day 1 of the next, and must be a whole number from 20 to 45 days.
  • The fertile window is seven days because sperm can survive for several days in the reproductive tract, so intercourse before ovulation can still lead to pregnancy. The egg itself survives roughly 12 to 24 hours after release.
  • Period days are an assumption. The calendar shades the first five days after your LMP as bleeding days; your own period may be shorter or longer.
  • All date maths uses whole UTC calendar days, so daylight-saving changes can never shift a result by a day.

Dates that do not exist, such as 30 February, and dates in the future are rejected rather than silently adjusted.

How to read your results

  • Ovulation day is the single best estimate of when an egg is released. It is a midpoint of a range, not a switch that flips on that morning.
  • Fertile window is the seven-day stretch when intercourse can lead to pregnancy. The days before ovulation carry the highest chance.
  • Peak fertility days are the two days immediately before ovulation, when conception is most likely.
  • Next period is a simple projection: your LMP plus one cycle length. If it does not arrive roughly on time, that is the moment a pregnancy test becomes meaningful.
  • Pregnancy test from is 14 days after the estimated ovulation day — late enough that the hormone hCG would normally be detectable. Testing earlier can produce a false negative.

Body signs such as cervical mucus changes, basal body temperature and ovulation predictor kits can all add information that a calendar cannot. None of them is required to use this page, but they are worth discussing with a clinician if you are tracking carefully.

How accurate is a calendar-based ovulation estimate?

Calendar prediction is a model, not a measurement. It assumes your cycle is regular and that your luteal phase is close to 14 days. Real cycles shift: stress, illness, travel and hormonal contraception can all move ovulation by several days in either direction.

That is why the fertile window here is deliberately generous. If your cycles are irregular, shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or if you have a condition such as polycystic ovary syndrome, treat the dates as a rough guide only. A clinician can offer tests and advice that a calculator cannot.

When to talk to a clinician

Contact a doctor or sexual health clinic if any of the following applies to you:

  • You have been trying to conceive for 12 months without success, or 6 months if you are 35 or older.
  • Your cycles are consistently shorter than 21 days, longer than 35 days, or very unpredictable.
  • You have severe pain with periods, very heavy bleeding, or bleeding between periods.
  • You have a positive pregnancy test together with pain, bleeding or dizziness.
  • You need contraception — do not use this page to avoid pregnancy.
Not medical advice. Early Pregnancy Tools is an educational calculator. It cannot confirm ovulation, diagnose a fertility problem, or replace a consultation. Always speak to a clinician about your own cycle and your own health.

Frequently asked questions

How does this ovulation calculator work?

It counts backwards from the end of your cycle. Ovulation is estimated as the first day of your last period plus your cycle length minus 14 days, because the luteal phase after ovulation is usually close to 14 days long.

How long is my fertile window?

This calculator shows a 7-day fertile window: the five days before ovulation, ovulation day itself and the day after. Sperm can survive for several days inside the body, which is why the days before ovulation matter most.

Why are the two peak days before ovulation?

Pregnancy is most likely after intercourse in the two days before ovulation. This calculator marks ovulation minus 2 days and ovulation minus 1 day as the peak fertility days.

Can I use this to avoid pregnancy?

No. This is an educational estimate, not a contraceptive method. Cycles vary from month to month and ovulation can come early or late, so do not rely on calendar predictions to prevent pregnancy. Talk to a clinician about contraception that suits you.
